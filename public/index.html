What the public mock board shows—and what it does not

ADP

ESPN only, without substitutions

ADP comes directly from ESPN Fantasy Football’s averageDraftPosition value. The table does not blend ESPN with consensus ADP, expert rank, or this league’s keeper-compressed board. An em dash means ESPN did not publish a usable value for that player.

2026 PROJECTED STATISTICS

Raw opportunity and production—not a private fantasy score

Season totals are divided by the provider’s projected games to create the compact per-game lines shown in the player table. Passing, rushing, receiving, kicking, and team-defense categories are displayed as raw statistics. The site does not convert them into NYFL fantasy points or a proprietary player grade.

AUTOMATIC CPU PICKS

The original strategic simulation engine

Automatic mode ports the CPU logic used by the original private draft board. Each opponent receives a seeded strategy—Balanced, RB Aggressor, WR Wave, Zero-RB, Late-QB, or Upside Hunter—and a controlled chalk-to-creative score. Candidates always begin in ascending ESPN ADP order, then a small viable market window is weighted by roster needs and strategy. Guardrails prevent a third RB or WR in Rounds 1–3, a second QB or TE through Round 5, require a QB and TE by their late-round deadlines, and delay D/ST and K until the final phase. Manual mode makes no automated selections.

Changing the team you control clears every mock pick and returns the order to overall #1. CPU mode is selected before the draft, and no automated pick runs until Start draft is pressed.
